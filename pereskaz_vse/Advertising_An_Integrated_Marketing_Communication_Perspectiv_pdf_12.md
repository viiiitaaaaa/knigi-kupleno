### Brand Name – Choosing a Brand Name  
*Choosing a brand name for a product is important from a positioning and an IMC perspective because the name communicates attributes and meaning.* (стр. 312)  

- Marketers look for names that **communicate product concepts** and help **position the product** in customers’ minds.  
- Examples that clearly communicate benefits: **WeChat**, **Pampers**, **Head & Shoulders** (стр. 312).  
- Example of a less descriptive name: **Tiger beer** – may reflect cultural heritage or feeling after drinking (стр. 312).

### Brand Symbol  
*Brand symbols (trademarks, “the mark of a trade”) can say more than words.* (стр. 312)  

- A **logo** is a trademark that appears in all brand communication.  
- Well‑designed logos are **distinctive, simple and fit the desired image and positioning** (стр. 312).  
- Example: **Apple’s stylised apple with a bite** – instantly recognisable worldwide (стр. 312).  

### Packaging  
*Packaging is a key branding element; it is often the consumer’s first exposure to the product.* (стр. 312‑313)  

- Traditional functions: **economy, protection, storage**.  
- Modern challenges: **self‑service retail, online shopping, click‑and‑collect** (стр. 312).  
- **≈75 % of supermarket purchases are unplanned** – packaging must attract attention (стр. 312).  
- A typical supermarket carries **>30 000 items** competing for attention (стр. 312).  
- Packaging must:  
  1. **Attract and hold consumer attention**.  
  2. **Communicate usage, composition, legal disclosures**.  
  3. **Possibly contain sales‑promotion messages** (contest, sweepstake, premium) (стр. 312).  
- Design factors influencing appeal: **size, shape, colour, lettering** – as important as advertising in moving product from shelf to checkout (стр. 312).  
- Example: **Miss Dior Blooming Bouquet perfume packaging** emphasises floral, feminine image and reinforces luxury (стр. 313).

### LO 5.6 – Measuring the Brand  

#### Brand Audit  
*Audits are a common starting point for planning; a brand audit determines what a brand means to its stakeholders.* (стр. 314)  

- **“Getting to the heart of a brand”** (Schultz & Schultz) – process (Figure 5.11) that:  
  1. **Identifies required stakeholder groups** for consultation.  
  2. **Explores key marketing and branding issues** with each group.  
  3. **Defines core values, core competencies, brand aspirations** for each stakeholder.  
  4. **Combines them into a statement of identity, personality, brand voice → brand promise** (стр. 314).

### Brand Equity  
*Brand equity is an intangible asset of added value or goodwill resulting from favourable image, differentiation, and consumer attachment.* (стр. 315)  

- **Kevin Keller definition**: *“the differential effect that brand knowledge has on consumer response to the marketing of the brand.”* (стр. 315)  
- **Schultz & Schultz simplification**: *“Brand equity encompasses both the perceptual values attached to a brand … and the financial values that result from positive consumer brand behaviours.”* (стр. 315)  

#### Aaker’s Five Elements of Brand Equity (Figure 5.12)  
1. **Brand‑name awareness**  
2. **Brand associations**  
3. **Perceived brand quality**  
4. **Proprietary brand assets** (trademarks, patents)  
5. **Brand loyalty**  

- The first four **directly influence ultimate brand loyalty** (стр. 315).  
- IMC directly impacts **brand‑name awareness, brand associations, perceived quality** (стр. 315).  

- **Negative brand equity** can arise from unplanned communication (newspaper stories, social‑media rumours) that harms attitude, behaviour, financial success (стр. 315).

### Brand‑Value Rankings (BrandZ)  
*Combining brand‑equity data with valuation analysis to rank the most valuable global brands.* (стр. 316)  

- **2018 BrandZ Top 100 total value:** **$4.4 trillion** (стр. 316).  
- **Top 10 global brands** (technology): **Google, Apple, Amazon** (стр. 316).  
- **Australian Top 40** – banks dominate Top 20 (≈ ½ of total value) (стр. 316).  
- **Other top Australian sectors:** Telecom (Telstra, Optus, Foxtel), Retail (Woolworths, Coles, Westfield, Bunnings, BWS), Energy (Origin, AGL), plus **Airlines (Qantas), Alcohol (Foster’s), Insurance (NRMA)** (стр. 316).  

### Brand Utility  
*The value a brand creates for consumers – “overall utility that the consumer associates with the use and consumption of the brand.”* (стр. 317)  

- **Benjamin Palmer (The Barbarian Group):** *“Brand utility is where the brand creates a commitment to a relationship… something useful in your life.”* (стр. 317)  
- **Result:** Consideration of a brand in purchase decisions **↑ 20 % since 2006** (стр. 317).  
- **Consumer return:** advocacy, wearing logo, repeated loyalty (стр. 317).  

- **Case Example:** **Westpac (New Zealand) – CashNav app** provides utility, becoming “the hottest seller at the app store” (стр. 317, Exhibit 5.18).  

### LO 5.7 – How Consumers Engage with Brands  

#### Brand Image (Consumer Perspective)  
*Brand image is the consumer‑held concept of a brand – a subjective, perceptual phenomenon formed through interpretation.* (стр. 318)  

- **Dobni & Zinkhan (1990):**  
  - “Brand image is … held by the consumer.”  
  - “Subjective, perceptual; formed via reasoned or emotional interpretation.”  
  - “Not inherent in technical/functional attributes; shaped by marketing, context, perceiver characteristics.” (стр. 318)  

- **Du Plessis (The Advertised Mind):** Perceptions = reality; emotions drive rationality; brand = consumer’s gut feeling (стр. 318).  

#### Emotional Bonding with Brands (Figure 5.13)  
*Three levels of consumer‑brand relationship:* (стр. 319‑320)  

1. **Product‑benefit level** – rational learning, information delivery; low loyalty, frequent switching.  
2. **Brand‑personality level** – consumer assigns personality (e.g., self‑assured, aggressive vs. timid) based on overt/covert cues in advertising.  
3. **Emotional‑attachment level** – strongest bond; positive psychological movement toward brand.  

- **Goal:** Develop greatest emotional linkage via advertising (стр. 319‑320).  

- **Case Study – Mastercard “Priceless” Campaign:**  
  - Situation: Mastercard was third‑most‑used card.  
  - Objective: Re‑position and create emotional bond while retaining functional appeal.  
  - Execution: Shopping‑list ads showing items purchasable for a set amount vs. one “priceless” item; tagline *“There are some things money can’t buy. For everything else there’s Mastercard.”* (стр. 319‑320).  
  - Integrated elements: sports sponsorships (All Blacks, Australian Open), contests, sweepstakes.  
  - Reach: **112 countries, 53 languages**, multiple awards (стр. 319‑320).  
  - Result: Brand value rose from **rank 99 (2008) to 15 (2018) → $70.8 billion** (стр. 320, Exhibit 5.16).  

#### Behavioural Bonding with Brands (Figure 5.14)  
*Four key behavioural links connecting company to customer* (стр. 320‑321):  

1. **Consistency** – consistent positioning, messages, service (core IMC concept).  
2. **Accessibility** – easy, quick contact; includes feedback loop.  
3. **Responsiveness** – good customer service; builds trust; supported by a database (transactional memory).  
4. **Commitment** – perception of long‑term relationship focus, not just quick sale.  

- These build **trust, liking, customer satisfaction** (стр. 321).  

#### Brand Loyalty Model (Kim, Morris & Swait, 2008) – Figure 5.15  
- **Brand credibility → cognitive conviction (thinking) + affective conviction (feeling)** (стр. 321).  
- Conviction influences **attitude strength**, which drives **brand commitment**, leading to **brand loyalty** (стр. 321).  

#### Digital Bonding with Brands (Figures 5.15‑5.16)  
- **Mapping consumer digital lives (Figure 5.16)** helps identify engagement opportunities (стр. 322).  

- **Martin & Todorov’s Five Levels of Digital Engagement** (стр. 323‑324):  
  1. **Attention‑getting** – disrupt, make consumer laugh.  
  2. **Utility** – task‑oriented tools (calculator, recommendation engine, price finder).  
  3. **Social connectivity** – turn consumers into brand ambassadors via social tools.  
  4. **Brand customisation** – personal ownership via custom content, expressing individuality.  
  5. **Brand lifestyle** – consumers “live” the brand; everyday services, utilities, social ecosystems create a movement.  

- **Example – Under Armour:**  
  - Developed a **fitness system** of interconnected apps tracking fitness, health, activity, food.  
  - Online community enables users to **connect, share goals, stay focused**, challenging Nike & Adidas (стр. 323‑324).  

### Online Brand Communities  

#### Core Characteristics (Muñiz Jr & O’Guinn, 2001)  
1. **Consciousness of kind** – collective sense of belonging.  
2. **Shared rituals and traditions**.  
3. **Moral responsibility/duty** to community and members (стр. 324).  

#### Member Roles (Kozinets)  
- **Tourists:** low brand involvement, weak social ties.  
- **Minglers:** strong social ties, low brand knowledge.  
- **Devotees:** high brand involvement, weak social ties.  
- **Insiders:** high brand involvement, strong social ties (стр. 324‑325).  

- **Power dynamics:** As proficiency or social ties increase, roles shift; power rises from **tourist (none)** to **insider (high control, advocacy)** (стр. 325).  

- **Power strategies:**  
  1. **Information**  
  2. **Aggregation**  
  3. **Participation**  
  4. **Controlling the relationship** (brand & community)  
  5. **Self‑liberation** (стр. 325).  

#### Community Examples  
- **World of Warcraft (Chinese site)** – 5‑6 million subscribers; strong rituals, traditions from game (стр. 326‑327).  
- **Lego Life** – social network for kids; avatars, sharing creations, likes from fans & Lego characters (стр. 326‑327).  

#### Impact of Participation  
- Participation **increases positive attitudes**, **brand identification**, **trust**, **brand loyalty**, and **eWOM intention** (стр. 326‑327).  

### Bonding, Involvement and Engagement  

#### From Involvement to Engagement  
- **Engagement is “the new black.”** (Paul Fisher, IAB) – high industry focus on measurement (стр. 326‑327).  

- **Early definition (psychological process):** thinking vs. feeling.  
- **Later addition (relationship/interactive/behavioural)** – accommodates online behaviour, makes engagement measurable (стр. 327).  

- **Advertising Research Foundation (ARF) 2006 definition:**  
  *“Turning on a prospect to a brand idea enhanced by the surrounding content.”*  
  - **Key antecedents:** **personal relevance** (consumer’s self‑identity, goals, values, outcomes) + **context** (situation, decision factor) (стр. 327‑328).  

- **Engagement can be with:** brand, product, message, or medium; leads to loyalty & advocacy (стр. 328).  

#### Hakuhodo Engagement Ring™ (Figure 5.18)  
- **Four rings of engagement:**  
  1. **Emotion** (central core)  
  2. **Feeling** (surrounds emotion)  
  3. **Choosing**  
  4. **Sharing**  
  5. **Committing** (outermost)  
- Emphasis on **personal relevance** across optimal touchpoints, intensity, order (стр. 327‑328).  

### IMC Turns Consumer Data into Brand Relationships  

#### Transactional Data  
- Captured at **point of transaction**: scanner data, payment options, membership cards (стр. 329).  
- Includes **demographic, geographic, purchase history, response lists, magazine subscriptions, association lists** (стр. 329).  

#### From Transactions to Relationships  
- **Transaction → relationship:** e.g., buying shoes (transaction) vs. posting photo on Instagram and receiving response (relationship) (стр. 330).  
- **Christian Groenroos:** transactions = price, short‑term; relationships = value, long‑term (стр. 330).  

#### Relational Database (Peltier et al., 2003)  
- Defined as **interactive psychographics** – values, motivations, beliefs, attitudes, lifestyles captured via database to nurture individualized buyer‑seller relationships (стр. 329‑330).  

#### Example – Bank Australia  
- **Challenge:** Find new customers like existing ones after re‑branding (customer‑owned bank).  
- **Approach:** Overlay internal database with **Roy Morgan Helix Personas** (psychographic, attitudinal, behavioural) (стр. 331).  
- **Outcome:** Data informed IMC objectives, strategy, tactics, creative decisions, media choices targeting socially aware audience (стр. 331, Exhibit 5.21).  

#### Benefits of a Robust Database (стр. 332)  
- **Understanding customers** (who they are, values, lifestyle, buying habits).  
- **Detect market changes** (taste, interests, touchpoint behaviour).  
- **Measure IMC campaign effectiveness** (which approaches work).  
- **Organisational memory** → better targeting, retention, loyalty.  

### LO 6.1 – IMC Ethics  

#### Ethics vs. Legality  
- **Ethics = moral principles** governing actions; an action may be legal yet unethical (стр. 332).  
- **Example:** Targeting “junk‑food” ads to children – legally permissible but many deem unethical due to obesity concerns (стр. 332).  
- **Other examples:** Stereotyped ads to ethnic groups (стр. 332).  

#### Controversial Advertising to Children  
- **Issues:** Age at which children differentiate ads from programming, ability to understand intent, impact on obesity.  
- **Case:** Fast‑food (McDonald’s, KFC) and unhealthy snack (Kellogg’s) ads; **Parents’ Voice** campaigns to ban such ads (стр. 332‑333, Exhibit 6.1).  

### LO 6.2 – Social and Ethical Criticisms of Advertising  

#### Common Criticisms  
- **Deceptive / Untruthful:** Misleading claims, puffery, false statements.  
- **Offensive / In bad taste:** Sexually suggestive, violent, discriminatory content.  
- **Exploitive:** Targeting vulnerable groups (women, children).  
- **Influence on values/lifestyles:** Perpetuating stereotypes, media control (стр. 334‑335).  

#### Evidence of Deception  
- Studies show **< 25 % of TV commercials are perceived as honest**; consumers rely mainly on **word‑of‑mouth** (стр. 334).  
- **Regulatory requirement:** Advertisers must have reasonable basis, provide evidence for performance claims (стр. 334).  
- **Notable cases:**  
  - **Ribena (GSK):** Claimed “four times the vitamin C of oranges”; later admitted misleading (стр. 334).  
  - **Nurofen Specific Pain (Reckitt Benckiser, Australia):** Misleading “specific pain” claims; admitted contravention of Australian Consumer Law (стр. 334).  

#### Ethical Debate  
- **Pro‑informative stance:** Ads should be primarily factual; avoid puffery.  
- **Pro‑advocacy stance:** Advertisers may present most favourable case; consumers can protect themselves; existing regulations suffice (стр. 334‑335).  

### AANA Advertiser Code of Ethics (Australia) – Section Summaries  

**Section 1 – Competitor Complaints** (стр. 335)  
1.1 Comply with Commonwealth and State/Territory law.  
1.2 No misleading or deceptive content.  
1.3 No misrepresentation harming competitor’s business or goodwill.  
1.4 No exploitation of environmental concerns with misleading claims.  
1.5 No misleading claims about Australian origin or content.  

**Section 2 – Consumer Complaints** (стр. 335)  
2.1 No depiction that discriminates or vilifies on race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, age, sexual preference, religion, disability, mental illness, political belief.  
2.2 No sexual appeal involving minors or exploitative/degrading portrayal.  
2.3 No unjustified violence portrayal.  
2.4 Sensitive treatment of sex, sexuality, nudity appropriate to audience.  
2.5 Use language appropriate to circumstances and audience; avoid strong/obscene language.  
2.6 No material contrary to prevailing community standards on health and safety.  
2.7 Advertisements must be clearly distinguishable as such to the audience.  

**Section 3 – Other Codes** (стр. 335)  
- The Code of Ethics is the overarching standard for advertising or marketing communications.
